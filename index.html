What we put in writing

Promises are cheap. These four are in the contract.

Every provider on your shortlist will tell you they answer quickly and price fairly. The question worth asking is which of them will sign it. These four go into your agreement, in the same document as the price.

In the contract

A person answers

Call during business hours and you reach an engineer, not a menu, not a queue, not a promise to call back. Fifteen minutes is the target on anything critical, and emergencies are covered 24/7/365.

In the contract

The number is the number

One flat monthly rate for the scope written into the agreement, with security and backup inside it. Projects — migrations, AI automation and workflows, a new office — are scoped and priced before work starts. Nothing outside the scope is ever billed without a quote first, and there is no annual escalator that appears at renewal.

In the contract

Your environment stays yours

Licences in your tenant, data under your ownership, documentation you can take with you. If you ever leave, you get a clean handover, because a provider who makes leaving painful has stopped competing on quality.

In the contract

We say what we do not know

If something is outside our depth, we say so and find the person whose depth it is. The alternative, learning on your outage, is how small providers lose clients they deserved to keep.

None of this is unusual to promise. Putting it in the agreement is the part that costs something, which is exactly why it is the part worth checking — with us and with everyone else you are talking to.

Questions we get on the first call

What does it cost?
A flat rate per user per month, and the honest answer is that the number depends on how many servers you run, what shape the environment is in, and what compliance you carry. We will not publish a figure that turns out to be wrong for you on the second call. The assessment gives you a real number in writing, and it costs nothing. Projects sit outside that rate — migrations, AI automation and workflows, new-office builds are scoped and priced before work starts, never billed afterwards.
Are we locked into a long contract?
Twelve months is standard, because onboarding a new client properly is real work that we do before any of it is paid for. What you will not find is an automatic escalator, a notice period designed to be missed, or an exit that costs you your own documentation.
We already have a provider. How does switching work?
Slowly and deliberately, which is the only way it goes well. We take an inventory first, stand up monitoring and backup alongside what you have, and only then move the pieces across. Most switches take about thirty days, and your current provider does not need to be told until you are ready to tell them.
Do you work with a company our size?
Ten to a hundred people is where we do our best work. Below ten, the fixed parts of what we do cost more than they return for you and we will say so. Above a hundred, you are usually better served by a co-managed arrangement alongside your own IT lead, which we also do.
Is security genuinely included, or is it an upsell?
Endpoint protection, identity protection across Microsoft 365, email filtering and backup are in the base plan. Some things genuinely are separate, such as a formal penetration test or a full SOC 2 readiness project, and we will tell you which is which before you sign rather than after.
We have an IT person already. Are you replacing them?
Not unless that is what you want. The more common arrangement is co-managed: your person keeps the wheel and the relationships, and we take the night coverage, the specialist work and the queue that never quite empties. The split of duties goes in writing so nobody is guessing whose job something is.
