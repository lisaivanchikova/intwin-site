How support actually works

The help desk is the part of managed IT your staff experience every week, and it is where most providers are quietly worst. Three decisions make the difference, and all three cost the provider something, which is why they are not universal.

Call and a person answers

No menu, no ticket first

During support hours you reach an engineer directly. The ticket is created afterwards by us, because making a person describe their problem into a form before anyone will speak to them is a way of managing our workload with their time.

Named engineers

The same faces

A small group who work with you regularly and know that the machine in the corner runs the thing the whole business depends on. Familiarity is not a nicety here; it is most of what makes a fast answer possible.

Response targets that mean something

Fifteen minutes on critical

Defined by impact rather than by who shouts, agreed at the start, and reported against honestly, including the months we miss. A target nobody measures is decoration.

Several ways in

Whatever suits the moment

Phone, email or the portal. Some people will always ring; some would rather type at midnight. Both are fine and both create the same ticket.

Starters and leavers

A template, not a scramble

Request a starter and they have a machine, an account and the right access on day one. Request a leaver and everything is closed the same day, with a record.

Application support

Not only Microsoft

Your practice management system, the AMS platform, the tax software. We will not pretend to be the vendor's own engineers, but we own the relationship and chase them so your staff do not have to.

A real escalation path

Names, not tiers

Who a problem goes to when the first engineer cannot solve it, how long that takes, and who you call when you think it is not moving. Published rather than implied.

Monthly reporting

Numbers you can act on

Volume, response times, what broke repeatedly, and what we suggest doing about the pattern rather than the individual tickets.

Worth knowing

What fifteen minutes actually means

It is a target for a human being making contact about a critical issue during support hours, and critical is defined in the agreement rather than in the moment: a person or a system that cannot work, with money or a client relationship attached. It is not a promise that everything is fixed in fifteen minutes, and any provider implying otherwise is selling you a number they cannot honour.

For everything else there are slower and equally explicit targets, because a printer that jams intermittently is a genuine annoyance and not an emergency, and treating both the same is how the actual emergency ends up waiting behind it.

Outside support hours, the emergency line reaches an on-call engineer every day of the year. We would rather you called and it turned out to be nothing than sat on something at ten at night trying to decide whether it counted.

Questions about help desk

Do we have to log a ticket before someone will help?
No. Ring the number and an engineer answers during support hours. We create the ticket afterwards, because the record exists for reporting and continuity rather than as a toll gate on getting help.
What counts as critical?
It is written into the agreement rather than judged in the moment. Broadly, it is a person or a system that cannot work, with money or a client relationship attached: the whole office is offline, the practice management system is down, or someone senior cannot present to a client in ten minutes. Being clear about the boundary in advance is what keeps the fast lane fast.
Is support in the United States?
Yes. The engineers your team speaks to work US hours in US time zones, and the emergency rota is staffed the same way. It is one of the reasons we are not the cheapest option per seat, and we would rather be straightforward about that trade-off.
What if the same problem keeps happening?
Then the ticket is a symptom and we have failed to look at the cause, which is what the monthly report exists to catch. Recurring tickets are grouped and reviewed, and a fix is proposed for the pattern. Being paid a flat rate means a problem that recurs costs us rather than earning us anything.
